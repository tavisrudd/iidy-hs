*account-number masking.* Rust: =mask_aws_account_numbers=
(=src/demo.rs:64-72=) applies =\b(\d{12})\b= (=demo.rs:22-23=) and then an
ARN pattern =(arn:aws:[^:]*:[^:]*:)(\d{12})([:/])= (=demo.rs:25-27=). The
ARN pass can never change anything the first pass left, because =:= and =/=
are word boundaries, so Rust's observable result equals Haskell's single
=\b[0-9]{12}\b= pass (=Demo.hs:288-296=). C11's note that Haskell has "a
plain 12-digit mask" is a difference in structure, not in output.
TypeScript: no masking and no =--mask-secrets= option (no match in =src/=).
Rust masks only in PTY mode, line by line (=demo.rs:303-340=); Haskell masks
the whole captured stdout and stderr separately (=Demo.hs:226-241=). That
streaming difference is out of scope here.

*sleep rounding.* Rust: =sleep: u64= (=demo.rs:43=), scaled duration
=(secs as f64 * timescaling) as u64= whole seconds, truncating
(=demo.rs:134-136=); the typing delay is =(50.0 * timescaling) as u64=
milliseconds, truncating (=demo.rs:363-366=). TypeScript:
=timeout(seconds * 1000 * timescaling)= and =timeout(50 * timescaling)=
(=src/demo.ts:123,144=), a =setTimeout= with a fractional millisecond value,
so effectively exact. Haskell: =DemoSleep !Int= parsed with =truncate n=
(=Demo.hs:149=), delay =round (secs * timescaling * 1e6)= microseconds
(=Demo.hs:197=) and typing delay =round (50 * timescaling * 1000)=
microseconds (=Demo.hs:261=). Rust and TypeScript disagree, so the choice
is Tavis's (see the Stop rule). Haskell is nearer TypeScript.
